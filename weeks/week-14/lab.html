Safety and verification lab

Two parts of this interactive lab are virtual laboratories in three dimensions (3D) with the hardware in the foreground. Part A tests the transfer logic of the island controller on a test bench. Part C chooses the design of the island supply in the electrical room and simulates 50,000 years of outages. Part B computes the losses of the critical supply step by step, and Part D asks for three values by hand. Part E is a field task at the battery inverter under the five safety rules. Part E is a field task at the battery inverter under the five safety rules. In the calculator every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A and C can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each laboratory first shows its site. Start moves the view closer, and the view returns to the site at the end. The laboratories work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: The transfer test bench

The transfer test bench checks the transfer logic before it goes into the electrical room, as in a factory acceptance test. The two real island controllers sit on the rail between simulators. A grid simulator stands for the city grid and an inverter simulator for the inverter of the battery. A breaker simulator stands for the breaker Q0 at the point of common coupling. A beacon warns if the inverter energises the grid. A screen shows the trace of every test in steps of 100 ms.

Choose the version of the transfer logic and a fault below the view, and press Run the test. Your missions: First, run the first version without a fault. Second, find the fault with which the first version forms the grid while the breaker is closed. Third, run the same fault with the corrected version and check that it stops safely.

Part B: The losses of the critical supply, step by step

The calculator follows the fault tree of the lecture for the critical supply of the campus. The battery management system blocks with 0.0040, the inverter is under repair with 0.0082 and the breaker fails to open with 0.0030, and two outages a year need the island. The calculator computes the controller pair, the survival of an outage, the probability of a loss, the losses per year and the years between them.

Part C: The electrical room

The electrical room of the campus holds the island supply of the critical loads. It has the breaker at the point of common coupling, the cabinets of the island controllers, the inverter of the battery and a board with the fault tree. Each design sets the reserve rule of the battery, the number of island controllers and the interval of their proof tests, and it has a yearly cost. The target of the campus is less than 0.05 losses of the critical supply per year.

Choose a design below the view and press Simulate. The board shows the fault tree of the design and 50,000 simulated years of outages. Your missions: First, simulate the current design without the reserve rule, with two controllers and yearly proof tests. Second, find a design below the target. Third, find the cheapest design below the target.

Part D: By hand: Stage 14 of the microgrid

A part has a failure rate of 0.1 per year: Compute its reliability after 5 years. A part fails on average once a year, a mean time to failure (MTTF) of 8760 h, and its repair takes 24 h: Compute its availability. Three parts with a reliability of 0.9 each vote two out of three: Compute the reliability of the vote.

Part E: Field task: Replacing the fan of the inverter

The inverter of the battery reports a failed fan in its power section, and an engineer replaces it. The inverter hangs on the breaker Q3 of its alternating-current (AC) side and on the switch S1 of its direct-current (DC) side. The work follows the five safety rules of the lecture. The buttons below are the actions at the switchgear, at the inverter and at the place of work. The two-pole voltage tester shows each measurement, and the label on the inverter names its supplies.
