Forecasting and anomaly lab

Two parts of this interactive lab are virtual laboratories in three dimensions (3D) with the hardware in the foreground. Part A forecasts the test fortnight in the metering room of the campus. Part C watches the solar plant for faults. Part B computes the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part D asks for the values of stage 10 by hand. Part E is a field task with the pyranometer and the data logger of the solar plant. In the calculator every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A and C can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each laboratory first shows its site. Start moves the view closer, and the view returns to the site at the end. The laboratories work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: The metering room

The metering room of the energy centre holds the smart meters of the five buildings and the data concentrator, which collects their readings every hour. Next to them stand the edge server, which computes the forecast, and the screen of the weather service with the temperature forecast of each day. A run forecasts the whole test fortnight from 13 to 26 April and replays it hour by hour on the large screen.

Choose the model, the calendar and the weeks of training below the view and press Run the forecast. The calendar and the weeks matter only for the two regressions. Your missions: First, run the forecasts yesterday and last week. Second, run a regression with the official calendar and one with weekdays only, and compare their errors on 23 April. Third, bring the root mean square error (RMSE) of the test fortnight below 17 kW.

Part B: The forecast and the alarm, step by step

It then computes the power that the solar plant should deliver at the measured irradiance and the standardised residual of the meter, with the decision of the threshold.

Part C: The solar plant

The solar plant of the campus has three rows of modules and two inverters. A pyranometer on its pole measures the irradiance, so the model of Week 1 knows what a healthy plant delivers in every hour. The monitoring cabinet compares this expectation with the meter of the plant and shows the standardised residual z, and its beacon lights up at every alarm. The screen shades the three faults of the maintenance log: An inverter trips, the meter freezes, and dust covers the modules.

Choose the detector, a threshold on z or the cumulative sum (CUSUM), and its settings below the view and press Run the fortnight. Your missions: First, run the threshold with k = 2 and with k = 3, and compare their false alarms. Second, find the smallest k, in steps of 0.5, without a false alarm. Third, find the dust on its first day, 20 April, without a false alarm.

Part D: By hand: Stage 10 of the microgrid

Three hours of the test fortnight have the actual load 512, 548 and 590 kW and the forecast 520, 540 and 572 kW: Compute the RMSE in kW. The load model of the campus has $a_8 = 490.45$ kW for a working day and $b = 5.54$ kW per kelvin: Compute its forecast for 08:00 with a forecast temperature of 2.0 °C. The model of the solar plant expects 280.0 kW, and the meter shows 266.0 kW: Compute $z$ with $\sigma = 1.35$ percent.

Part E: Field task: The pyranometer after its recalibration

The pyranometer of the solar plant in Part C is back from its recalibration and sits on its pole again. Since then, the residual of the plant alarms in every sunny hour, although the plant runs without faults. The data logger in the monitoring cabinet converts the voltage of the pyranometer into the irradiance, and the new calibration certificate lies on the desk.

The keys work as on the real device. The keys ▲ and ▼ choose a line, OK opens it or starts and saves a change, and the escape key (ESC) goes back. A number changes digit by digit: ◀ and ▶ choose the digit, and ▲ and ▼ change it. On a computer, the arrow keys, Enter and Escape work as well, once the front panel has been clicked.
